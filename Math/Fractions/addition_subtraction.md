# Fractions: Addition and Subtraction

## Fraction Terminology

A fraction represents part of a whole:

$$
\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}
$$

- **Numerator:** the number above the fraction line. It tells us how many parts we have.
- **Denominator:** the number below the fraction line. It tells us how many equal parts make one whole. It cannot be zero.

For example, in  $\frac{3}{5}$\, the numerator is 3 and the denominator is 5.

### Proper Fraction

A **proper fraction** has a numerator smaller than its denominator, so its value is less than \(1\).

$$
\frac{3}{5}
$$

### Improper Fraction

An **improper fraction** has a numerator equal to or greater than its denominator, so its value is at least \(1\).

$$
\frac{7}{4}
$$

### Mixed Number

A **mixed number** contains a whole number and a proper fraction.

$$
1\frac{3}{4}
$$

The mixed number \($1\frac{3}{4}$\) and the improper fraction \($\frac{7}{4}$\) have the same value.

To convert a mixed number to an improper fraction:

1. Multiply the whole number by the denominator.
2. Add the numerator.
3. Place the result over the original denominator.

$$
2\frac{1}{3}=\frac{(2\times3)+1}{3}=\frac{7}{3}
$$

## Cancelling and Simplifying

**Cancelling** means dividing the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero common factor. This does not change the value of the fraction.

For example, the HCF of \(18\) and \(24\) is \(6\):

$$
\frac{18}{24}
=\frac{18\div6}{24\div6}
=\frac{3}{4}
$$

A fraction is in its **simplest form** when its numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than \(1\).

> Cancel only common factors in a numerator and denominator. Do not cancel separate terms joined by \(+\) or \(-\).

## Adding Fractions

### Same Denominator

Add the numerators and keep the denominator:

$$
\frac{a}{c}+\frac{b}{c}=\frac{a+b}{c}
$$

### Different Denominators (Addition)

Find a common denominator, preferably the LCM. Convert the fractions to equivalent fractions, add the numerators, and simplify the result.

### Addition Example 1: Same Denominator

$$
\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2}{5}
=\frac{1+2}{5}
=\frac{3}{5}
$$

### Addition Example 2: Different Denominators

The LCM of \(4\) and \(6\) is \(12\):

$$
\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{6}
=\frac{3}{12}+\frac{2}{12}
=\frac{5}{12}
$$

### Addition Example 3: Mixed Numbers

First convert the mixed numbers to improper fractions:

$$
1\frac{1}{2}+2\frac{2}{3}
=\frac{3}{2}+\frac{8}{3}
$$

The LCM of \(2\) and \(3\) is \(6\):

$$
\frac{3}{2}+\frac{8}{3}
=\frac{9}{6}+\frac{16}{6}
=\frac{25}{6}
=4\frac{1}{6}
$$

## Subtracting Fractions

### Same Denominator (Subtraction)

Subtract the numerators and keep the denominator:

$$
\frac{a}{c}-\frac{b}{c}=\frac{a-b}{c}
$$

### Different Denominators (Subtraction)

Find a common denominator, convert the fractions to equivalent fractions, subtract the numerators, and simplify the result.

### Subtraction Example 1: Same Denominator

$$
\frac{7}{8}-\frac{3}{8}
=\frac{7-3}{8}
=\frac{4}{8}
=\frac{1}{2}
$$

### Subtraction Example 2: Different Denominators

The LCM of \(6\) and \(4\) is \(12\):

$$
\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{4}
=\frac{10}{12}-\frac{3}{12}
=\frac{7}{12}
$$

### Subtraction Example 3: Mixed Numbers

First convert the mixed numbers to improper fractions:

$$
3\frac{1}{4}-1\frac{2}{3}
=\frac{13}{4}-\frac{5}{3}
$$

The LCM of \(4\) and \(3\) is \(12\):

$$
\frac{13}{4}-\frac{5}{3}
=\frac{39}{12}-\frac{20}{12}
=\frac{19}{12}
=1\frac{7}{12}
$$

## Common Mistakes

- Do not add or subtract the denominators.
- Do not combine numerators until the denominators are equal.
- Keep the denominator unchanged when the denominators are already equal.
- Simplify the final answer where possible.
- Convert an improper final answer to a mixed number when required.
