# Fractions: Multiplication and Division

## Fraction Terminology

A fraction represents part of a whole:

$$
\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}
$$

- **Numerator:** the number above the fraction line. It tells us how many parts we have.
- **Denominator:** the number below the fraction line. It tells us how many equal parts make one whole. It cannot be zero.
- **Proper fraction:** the numerator is smaller than the denominator, such as \(\frac{2}{5}\).
- **Improper fraction:** the numerator is equal to or greater than the denominator, such as \(\frac{7}{4}\).
- **Mixed number:** a whole number and a proper fraction together, such as \(1\frac{3}{4}\).

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing:

$$
2\frac{1}{3}
=\frac{(2\times3)+1}{3}
=\frac{7}{3}
$$

## Cancelling

**Cancelling** means dividing a numerator and a denominator by the same non-zero common factor.

When multiplying fractions, we may cancel vertically or diagonally before multiplication because all numerators are factors of the final numerator and all denominators are factors of the final denominator.

For example:

$$
\frac{3}{4}\times\frac{8}{9}
$$

Cancel \(3\) with \(9\) by dividing both by \(3\), and cancel \(8\) with \(4\) by dividing both by \(4\):

$$
\frac{\cancel{3}^{1}}{\cancel{4}^{1}}
\times
\frac{\cancel{8}^{2}}{\cancel{9}^{3}}
=\frac{1\times2}{1\times3}
=\frac{2}{3}
$$

> Cancel only a numerator with a denominator. Both numbers must be factors in a multiplication.

## Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together:

$$
\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{c}{d}
=\frac{a\times c}{b\times d}
$$

A common denominator is not required.

### Multiplication Example 1: Proper Fractions

$$
\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{4}{5}
=\frac{2\times4}{3\times5}
=\frac{8}{15}
$$

### Multiplication Example 2: Cancelling First

Cancel the common factors before multiplying:

$$
\frac{3}{4}\times\frac{8}{9}
=\frac{1}{1}\times\frac{2}{3}
=\frac{2}{3}
$$

### Multiplication Example 3: Mixed Numbers

First convert the mixed numbers to improper fractions:

$$
1\frac{1}{2}\times2\frac{2}{3}
=\frac{3}{2}\times\frac{8}{3}
$$

Cancel \(3\) with \(3\), and cancel \(8\) with \(2\):

$$
\frac{3}{2}\times\frac{8}{3}
=\frac{1}{1}\times\frac{4}{1}
=4
$$

## Dividing Fractions

To divide by a fraction:

1. Keep the first fraction.
2. Change division to multiplication.
3. Invert the second fraction.
4. Multiply and simplify.

The inverted fraction is called the **reciprocal**.

$$
\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}
=\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{d}{c}
=\frac{a\times d}{b\times c}
$$

The divisor cannot be zero, so \(c\neq0\).

### Division Example 1: Proper Fractions

Invert the second fraction and multiply:

$$
\frac{3}{4}\div\frac{2}{5}
=\frac{3}{4}\times\frac{5}{2}
=\frac{15}{8}
=1\frac{7}{8}
$$

### Division Example 2: Cancelling First

$$
\frac{5}{6}\div\frac{10}{9}
=\frac{5}{6}\times\frac{9}{10}
$$

Cancel \(5\) with \(10\), and cancel \(9\) with \(6\):

$$
\frac{5}{6}\times\frac{9}{10}
=\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{3}{2}
=\frac{3}{4}
$$

### Division Example 3: Mixed Numbers

First convert the mixed numbers to improper fractions:

$$
2\frac{1}{4}\div1\frac{1}{2}
=\frac{9}{4}\div\frac{3}{2}
$$

Invert the second fraction, multiply, and cancel:

$$
\frac{9}{4}\times\frac{2}{3}
=\frac{3}{2}
=1\frac{1}{2}
$$

## Common Mistakes

- Do not find a common denominator for multiplication or division.
- Do not invert either fraction when multiplying.
- When dividing, invert only the second fraction.
- Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before calculating.
- Cancel only between a numerator and a denominator.
- Never divide by zero or use zero as a denominator.
- Simplify the final answer and convert an improper fraction to a mixed number when required.
